Screen
to pigment

A visitor comes once, so the home page should be one journey with a beginning and an end. It starts on the whole screen you design. It goes down through its components and tokens to a single pixel. The pixel becomes a drop of paint on paper. Then it comes back out to a screen where the visitor writes to you. Your site already has the first half of this story. These ideas finish it and give it depth.

Sketch 2 · stop 5

The pixel becomes pigment

The sand pixel from sketch 1 grows on its pixel grid, rounds into a drop and lands on paper. The Chenonceau watercolour blooms out of it in layers, with the darker wet edge that water leaves on paper. This is the turn from designer to painter, and it happens without a paragraph.

Says
The person who works to the pixel also paints with water. The hand is the same.
Built with
Canvas 2D: stacked, randomly deformed polygons at low opacity used as a mask, the technique Tyler Hobbs published for generative watercolour. About 150 lines.
In this sketch
Your scan from public/watercolours/architecture/france.

Sketch 3 · every click into a study

The cover becomes the case

Pick a tile. It lifts toward you, the other tiles fall back, and the cover grows into the case-study hero. “Work” brings it back to its place. On a site someone visits once, every click into a study feels like one continuous move.

Says
You chose this project, and you are carried into it.
Built with
The View Transitions API: a view-transition-name on the cover and the hero. React Router 6.26, which the site uses, has the unstable_viewTransition prop on Link. Browsers without it navigate as today.
In this sketch
Plain JavaScript animation standing in for the browser’s transition.

What I would leave out

Depth without the usual tricks

  • A three.js scene for the whole site. It adds several hundred kilobytes, and the text in your screens goes soft once it is a texture. CSS 3D keeps them as sharp images.
  • A 3D trophy for the two Golds. The award reads better as a line of text naming the jury’s category.
  • Custom cursors and blobs that follow the pointer.
  • Gyroscope tilt on phones. iOS asks the visitor for permission first, which is an odd first question on a portfolio.
  • Page-curl galleries for the watercolours.
  • Scroll-jacking. Pinning a section is fine. Changing the speed of the wheel is not.
  • Glass panels and blur on top of the 3D. Depth should come from layers and light.
  • Motion the visitor cannot stop. Every sketch here has a still for reduced motion and a scrub control.
